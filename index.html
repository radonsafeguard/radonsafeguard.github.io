Why It Matters

Radon Is the #2 Cause of Lung Cancer

Radon is an invisible, odorless radioactive gas that seeps into buildings through foundation cracks and gaps. Health Canada estimates radon exposure causes roughly 16% of lung cancer deaths in Canada. Testing is the only way to know your risk.

Elevated Levels Are Common

Many homes in higher-risk regions, including Alberta, can exceed Health Canada’s 200 Bq/m³ action level. Testing is the only way to know your property’s levels.

Common Questions About Radon

Quick answers for Edmonton and Alberta homeowners. For more detail, visit our full radon learning centre.

Radon is a colorless, odorless radioactive gas that seeps into buildings from the ground. Health Canada identifies it as the leading cause of lung cancer in non-smokers — testing is the only way to know if your property is affected.

There is no known safe concentration of radon. Health Canada recommends taking action to reduce levels when they reach 200 becquerels per cubic metre (Bq/m³) or higher. Risk still exists below that action level, and some homeowners choose to mitigate at lower readings for extra peace of mind.

Active Soil Depressurization (ASD) is the most effective and common method for reducing indoor radon levels. It works by creating a negative pressure beneath a building's foundation to reverse the natural flow of soil gas, preventing radon from entering the home.
