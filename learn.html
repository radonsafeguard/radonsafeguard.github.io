General Questions

Radon is a colorless, odorless radioactive gas that forms naturally when uranium breaks down in soil and rock. It can seep into buildings through foundation cracks, gaps around pipes, and other openings. Health Canada estimates radon causes roughly 16% of lung cancer deaths in Canada, making it the leading cause of lung cancer in non-smokers.

Alberta has some of the highest indoor radon levels in Canada, and many Edmonton-area homes can exceed common health-authority guidelines. No level of radon is risk-free. Levels vary by neighbourhood, geology, and even from house to house on the same street — testing is the only way to know your exposure.

Radon is typically highest on the lowest occupied level of a building, but elevated levels can be found on upper floors as well — especially in homes with HVAC systems that circulate air between floors. Slab-on-grade homes without basements can still have high radon levels. Testing should always be performed on the lowest lived-in level.

Testing

Radon tests are typically placed on the lowest occupied level of the home — for example, a finished basement or main floor if the basement is unfinished and unused. Placement follows Health Canada–aligned protocols so results reflect the air people actually breathe. Our professionals choose the best location for your layout during setup.

Short-term and long-term tests need different house conditions — for example, closed-house versus open-house protocols. We’ll recommend the right test for your situation and walk you through every requirement so results are as accurate as possible.

Timing depends on the type of test and how results are processed. After the test period ends, we review the data and provide a clear report with your levels and recommended next steps. We’ll set expectations for turnaround when we schedule your test.

There is no known safe level of radon — lower is always better. Leading organizations recommend taking action to reduce indoor levels at or above these reference points: Health Canada at 200 Bq/m³, the US EPA at 148 Bq/m³ (4 pCi/L), and the World Health Organization at 100 Bq/m³. Many homeowners still choose to mitigate at lower readings for extra peace of mind.

Health Canada recommends testing every two years, even if you previously had low results. Retest after any major renovation, foundation work, or HVAC changes. If you have a mitigation system installed, we recommend testing every two years to confirm the system continues to operate effectively.

If results are elevated relative to recommended action levels — or if you simply want to reduce exposure further — we’ll explain your options and, when appropriate, design a mitigation plan. You can start with a free quote or learn more about mitigation systems.

Mitigation

Active Soil Depressurization (ASD) is the most effective and common method for reducing indoor radon levels. It works by creating a negative pressure beneath a building's foundation to reverse the natural flow of soil gas, preventing radon from entering the home.

Pricing is determined by the complexity of each individual job. Factors include your home's foundation type, building size, radon levels, crawl space conditions, and the mitigation system design required. We provide a detailed written quote after assessing your property — at no obligation.

Modern mitigation systems are designed to be as unobtrusive as possible. Ventilation piping is typically routed to the exterior via the mechanical room. The fan is also typically mounted in the mechanical room and operates quietly — usually no louder than a refrigerator. Most homeowners barely notice it running.

Mitigation systems require minimal maintenance. Check the U-tube manometer (visual gauge) monthly to confirm the fan is running. Fans typically last 5–10 years before needing replacement. We include a post-mitigation verification test after mitigation, as well as recommend a follow-up inspection two years after installation.

Most residential active soil depressurization installs are completed in about one to two days, depending on foundation type, layout, and routing for the vent pipe. Real Estate & Commercial

Radon testing is not legally required in Alberta, but it is strongly recommended by Health Canada and most home inspectors during real estate transactions. Because radon is invisible and undetectable without professional equipment, testing is standard practice in the Edmonton market. We offer expedited testing designed for closing timelines.

This is negotiable between buyer and seller, similar to other repair items. In many markets, the seller installs mitigation before closing if elevated levels are found. In others, buyers negotiate a credit. We provide same-day mitigation quotes to help keep transactions on track, and we work directly with agents, attorneys, and lenders.

Yes. Health Canada recommends that all buildings — including offices, schools, and multi-unit properties — be tested for radon. Under Alberta's Occupational Health and Safety Code, employers and property managers have a duty to provide a safe environment for occupants.
